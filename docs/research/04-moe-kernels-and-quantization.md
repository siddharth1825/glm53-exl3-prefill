# MoE prefill GEMM strategies for a 288-expert, 4-bit trellis (EXL3) MoE on GB10 (sm_121)

Legend: **[F]** = fact from a cited primary source; **[I]** = my inference/estimate. Per-TP-rank, per-MoE-layer constants used throughout **[I, from your numbers]**: expert weights = 288 × (2·4096·1024 + 1024·4096) = 3.62 G params → 1.81 GB at 4 bpw (6.6 ms of DRAM at 273 GB/s), 3.62 GB as int8/fp8 (13.3 ms), 7.2 GB as fp16 (26.5 ms). FLOPs = 2·T·8·12.58 M = T × 201 MFLOP → 0.41 / 0.82 / 1.65 TFLOP at 2K / 4K / 8K tokens. Your current fused kernel therefore runs at ≈15 TFLOPS at every chunk size (14 ms @1K … 110 ms @8K), i.e. ~12% of bf16 peak; your Triton fp8 path hit 57 TFLOPS at 8K.

## 1. MoE prefill GEMM strategies (token-sort + grouped GEMM, Triton, FlashInfer/TRT-LLM, SGLang)

**Grouped-GEMM libraries.** DeepGEMM provides `m_grouped` contiguous (prefill) and masked (decode) FP8 GEMMs, but requires SM90 or SM100 and does not list SM120/121; contiguous layout needs each expert's M-segment aligned to the M block size (`get_mk_alignment_for_contiguous_layout()`) **[F]** ([DeepGEMM README](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepGEMM)). CUTLASS: SM120 (GeForce Blackwell) support landed in 3.9.0 (block-scaled NVFP4 dense/sparse/grouped examples), SM121 "DGX Spark" parity plus "SM120 mixed input blockscaled grouped GEMM" and a SM120 blockwise-FP8 GEMM (example 87) in 4.2.0, and a new grouped-GEMM style "aligned to torch's grouped_mm/scaled_grouped_mm interface" in 4.5.0 (May 2026) **[F]** ([CUTLASS changelog 4.3.2](https://docs.nvidia.com/cutlass/4.3.2/CHANGELOG.html), [latest changelog](https://docs.nvidia.com/cutlass/latest/CHANGELOG.html)). But as of CUTLASS 4.5 there is **no CollectiveBuilder for tensor/token-scaled FP8 grouped GEMM on SM_120/121**, so vLLM's FP8 MoE falls back to Triton on GB10 **[F]** ([vllm #43507](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/43507)). cuBLAS's `cublasGemmGroupedBatchedEx` covers FP16/BF16/FP32/FP64 only **[F]** ([NVIDIA blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/introducing-grouped-gemm-apis-in-cublas-and-more-performance-updates)); `torch._grouped_mm` is documented as SM90+ with no stated consumer-Blackwell support **[F]** ([PyTorch forum](https://discuss.pytorch.org/t/question-about-torch-grouped-mm-gpu-support/222470)).

**Triton fused_moe (vLLM).** `moe_align_block_size` pads each expert's token list to a multiple of `BLOCK_SIZE_M`; worst-case padded rows = `topk_ids.numel() + num_experts*(block_size-1)` **[F]** ([vLLM docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/v0.11.1/api/vllm/model_executor/layers/fused_moe/moe_align_block_size/)). Default configs: for fp8 block-quant, `BLOCK_SIZE_M` = 16 (M≤64) else 64, `BLOCK_K` = 128, `num_stages` 3–4; for bf16, `block_m` = 16/32/64/128 for M ≤ 32/96/512/else; `GROUP_SIZE_M` = 16 when tokens_per_expert > 128 (L2 reuse of B tiles across M-blocks) **[F]** ([fused_moe.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vllm-project/vllm/main/vllm/model_executor/layers/fused_moe/fused_moe.py)). **[I]** For E = 288 the expected padding is ≈ 288·(B_M−1)/2 rows: at 2K tokens (16.4K real rows) that is +55% extra tile-rows at B_M = 64 and +111% at B_M = 128; at 8K it is +14% / +28%. The SonicMoE paper quantifies the same "tile quantization" effect (up to ~20–25% wasted FLOPs at 256 experts, T=16K, K=4, M_tile=128) and proposes "token rounding" to remove it **[F]** ([SonicMoE, arXiv 2512.14080](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14080)). On SM121 SGLang's Triton MoE config selection picked a kernel needing 147,456 B of smem against a hardware limit of **101,376 B** **[F]** ([sglang #28019](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/issues/28019)), so any 4-stage 128×128×128 config is out.

**FlashInfer / TRT-LLM / SGLang.** On B200 (sm_100a) with a DeepSeek-like 256-expert top-8 config at 4096 tokens (≈128 rows/expert), NVFP4 MoE reaches only 1132 (FlashInfer), 993 (SGLang), 968 (vLLM) TFLOPS — vs 1262 TFLOPS for the 8-expert config — and the BF16 baseline is ≈240 TFLOPS **[F]** ([apsys blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/apsys/blackwell-nvfp4-comparison)). **[I]** That is ~10% of B200's bf16 peak and ~25% of its FP4 peak: with ~128 rows/expert even datacenter kernels are far from peak. SonicMoE's forward grouped GEMM reaches "88% of the upper bound (cuBLAS BMM…)" and >550 TFLOPS on H100 / >1100 on B300, 43% over DeepGEMM at fine granularity **[F]** ([SonicMoE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14080)). On GB10 specifically, a community CUTLASS-4.4+SM121 build measured **NVFP4 dense 356 TFLOPS (71% of 500), FP8 dense 188 TFLOPS, MoE grouped GEMM 120–154 TFLOPS** **[F]** ([NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/fp4-on-dgx-spark-why-it-doesnt-scale-like-youd-expect/360142)). FlashInfer's `b12x` CuTe-DSL fused NVFP4 MoE (dispatch + W1 + SwiGLU + W2) targets SM120/121 and was merged into vLLM on 2026‑05‑20, giving +1.8%/+6.0% throughput on Qwen3‑30B‑A3B‑NVFP4 on DGX Spark; it pins `nvidia-cutlass-dsl==4.4.2` because 4.5.0 "generates incorrect PTX for SM121" **[F]** ([vllm PR 40082](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/40082)). FlashInfer's SM121 audit: CUTLASS FP8 MoE supported, grouped GEMM has "correctness issues documented for num_groups > 1 on both SM120 and SM121", trtllm-gen MLA/decode SM100-only **[F]** ([flashinfer #3170](https://github.com/flashinfer-ai/flashinfer/issues/3170)). SGLang's DGX Spark tracker notes FP8 CUTLASS kernels "fail to dispatch on GB10" and were disabled **[F]** ([sglang #11658](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/issues/11658)); its SM120 plan ranks "CUTLASS grouped GEMM" highest priority **[F]** ([sglang #19637](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/issues/19637)).

## 2. Weight-only 4-bit kernels and what trellis decoders do at large M

**Marlin.** Dequantizes INT4→FP16 with bit tricks in registers, keeps the full batch tile of A in shared memory and streams weight sub-tiles through registers with a depth‑4 cp.async pipeline; it reaches ≈3.87× (ideal 4×) at batch ≤16–32, 1.5× at 128, and "is nearly identical to an uncompressed compute-bound matmul up to batch size 1024, with only ≈10% slow-down at even larger input shapes" (A100) **[F]** ([MARLIN, arXiv 2408.11743](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.11743)). **Machete** (Hopper): Marlin's `mma` path loses ~37% of Hopper peak vs `wgmma`; Machete pre-shuffles weights, uses TMA + warp specialization and is "competitive with FP16" at batch ≥128; Hopper-only **[F]** ([Red Hat/Neural Magic](https://developers.redhat.com/articles/2024/10/14/introducing-machete-mixed-input-gemm-kernel)). **MiLo** (3-bit MoE) beats Marlin by 16–24% at batch 16 and 1.26× at larger batches on A100 **[F]** ([MiLo](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.02658)).

**Mixed-input tensor cores on sm_120/121.** There is no INT4×BF16 MMA in hardware; every "mixed-input" kernel upconverts in registers. What sm_120a does add is `mma.sync…kind::f8f6f4` (m16n8k32) with A/B independently chosen from {e4m3, e5m2, e3m2, e2m3, e2m1}, plus `.block_scale` variants; e2m1/e3m2/e2m3 and `.kind/.block_scale` require sm_120a **[F]** ([PTX ISA](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/parallel-thread-execution/)). This is the basis of CUTLASS's "SM120 mixed input blockscaled grouped GEMM" (MXFP8 act × MXFP4/6 weights at FP8 rate) **[F]**. The NVFP4 path on SM12x is `mma.sync.aligned.kind::mxf4nvf4.block_scale.scale_vec::4X.m16n8k64` — warp-level register MMA, no TMEM, no WGMMA/tcgen05 **[F]** ([Colfax](https://research.colfax-intl.com/cutlass-tutorial-nvfp4-blockscaled-gemm-on-nvidia-rtx-pro-blackwell-gpus-sm12x/), [backend.ai](https://www.backend.ai/blog/2026-02-is-dgx-spark-actually-a-blackwell)).

**Trellis/QTIP decoders at large M.** QTIP's codes cost "≤ 4 instructions per weight" (1MAD/3INST; HYB with a 2 KiB L1 LUT); the released kernels are **batch‑1 matrix‑vector kernels** only ("2, 3, and 4 bit matrix-vector multiplication kernels for the HYB code") and the authors state QTIP "won't help if your inference is compute-bound" **[F]** ([QTIP paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.11235), [repo](https://github.com/Cornell-RelaxML/qtip), [Together blog](https://www.together.ai/blog/even-better-even-faster-quantized-llms-with-qtip)). I found **no published QTIP/TCQ kernel that decodes a tile once and reuses it across many activation rows**; the 2025–26 trellis follow-ups (BCJR‑QAT, HARP, ICQuant) are quantization methods, not kernels **[F/I]**. **exllamav3** itself: the fused `exl3_gemm` processes **exactly 16 A rows per block iteration** (`static_assert(TILESIZE_M == 16)`), decodes B via `dq_dispatch` straight into `mma.m16n8k16` fragments, 8 warps, and "B tiles are not reused across different A rows" **[F]** ([exl3_gemm_inner.cuh](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/turboderp-org/exllamav3/master/exllamav3/exllamav3_ext/quant/exl3_gemm_inner.cuh)). Its host code switches to reconstruct + GEMM above `AUTO_RECONSTRUCT_THRESHOLD = 144` rows, and for rows ≥ 1024 uses a fused reconstruct that emits `W = diag(suh)·H128·Ŵ·H128·diag(svh)` so a plain GEMM on raw input works ("breakeven is rows ~400–900") **[F]** ([exl3.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/turboderp-org/exllamav3/master/exllamav3/modules/quant/exl3.py), [reconstruct.cu](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/turboderp-org/exllamav3/master/exllamav3/exllamav3_ext/quant/reconstruct.cu)). For MoE, the fused `exl3_moe` kernel is used only when `num_tokens*top_k ≤ TEMP_ROWS_FUSED = 128`; larger expert assignments go to a per-expert graph up to `TEMP_ROWS_GRAPH`, beyond which the "DQ path" dequantizes the expert and runs a dense GEMM **[F]** ([block_sparse_mlp.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/turboderp-org/exllamav3/master/exllamav3/modules/block_sparse_mlp.py)). **[I]** So upstream exllamav3's answer to prefill is "dequant per expert per call" — exactly your DRAM-bound 35 ms path; nobody has shipped the Marlin-style weight-stationary trellis GEMM.

Other quantitative anchors **[F]**: DeepSeek‑V3 trained with E4M3, 1×128 activation tiles and 128×128 weight blocks and reports "relative loss error … consistently below 0.25%" vs BF16; H800 FP8 tensor cores retain "around 14 bits" of accumulation, so partials are promoted to FP32 every N_C = 128 ([DeepSeek-V3](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.19437)). QuaRot uses symmetric per-token activation scales with clipping ratio 0.9 and "a single on-the-fly Hadamard transform before quantizing" the down-proj input (≤7% overhead) ([QuaRot](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.00456)). SpinQuant shows activation kurtosis falling from ">200" to "≈3" after rotation, and its W4A8 results use only the *offline* rotations R1/R2 (R3/R4 online Hadamards are reserved for 4-bit activations) ([SpinQuant](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16406)). HadaCore (tensor-core Hadamard-128) gives 1.1–1.4× over Dao's kernel; FP8 attention with Hadamard recovers MMLU 65.09–65.45 vs 64.40 without ([PyTorch blog](https://pytorch.org/blog/hadacore/)).

**Is it valid to quantize activations in EXL3's rotated domain?** **[F]** Yes — QuaRot, SpinQuant, QuIP#, FlatQuant all quantize activations *after* the rotation; EXL3's y = ((x⊙su)H)·W'·(H⊙sv) means the operand of W' is precisely a rotated activation. **[I]** Two caveats specific to EXL3: (a) the rotation is a 128-point block Hadamard, so outlier energy is only spread within each 128-block — per-token-per-128-group scales (DeepSeek style) align exactly with that structure and are the right granularity; (b) su is per expert, so the rotated activation differs per (token, expert) and must be quantized in the gather step (8 rows/token; ≈2·T·8·4096 bytes — about 0.5 GB of traffic at 8K tokens, ~2 ms).

**Why your fp8 path shows 6.3%.** **[I]** E4M3 has 3 mantissa bits: relative rounding error per element is uniform-ish in ±2⁻⁴…2⁻³, ≈2.5–3.5% RMS; with both operands in fp8 the dot-product relative error is ≈√2 × that ≈ 4%. 6.3% suggests the activations were quantized in the *raw* (un-rotated) domain or with groups spanning outlier channels. INT8 per-token-per-128-group on Gaussian-like rotated data: step ≈ 2.75σ/127 → RMS error ≈ 0.6–0.9%; the trellis-decoded W' is itself near-Gaussian in the rotated domain (that is what incoherence processing guarantees), so int8 per-tensor/per-row W' costs ≈0.9%. **Expected W8A8-int8 output error ≈1.1–1.3% vs your 6.3% fp8** — consistent with QuaRot's rotated INT8 being lossless (5.50 vs 5.47) and QoQ's W4A8 adding only +0.05–0.15 ppl over W4A16. Crucially INT8 runs at the FP8 tensor-core rate on this architecture (below), so there is no speed penalty.

## 5. Dequant-once vs per-16-row decode: where each wins

**[I — model, using your measurements]**

| Chunk T | rows/expert (avg) | Current fused (16-row) | Dequant→fp16 + GEMM (35 + T/100 TFLOPS) | Dequant→int8 (≈20 ms) + int8 GEMM @150 TFLOPS | Weight-stationary trellis kernel (M_blk = 128, fp16 MMA) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1K | 28 | 14 ms | ≈37 ms | ≈21 ms | ≈9–11 ms |
| 2K | 57 | 24 ms | ≈39 ms | ≈23 ms | ≈10–13 ms |
| 4K | 114 | 54 ms | ≈43 ms | ≈26 ms | ≈16–22 ms |
| 8K | 228 | 110 ms | ≈52 ms | ≈31 ms | ≈25–35 ms |

Reasoning: the fused kernel's cost is ~7 ms per "pass" over all trellis weights (it needs ⌈rows_e/16⌉ passes per expert), so a kernel that decodes each 16×16 B tile into registers once and multiplies it against M_blk = 128 A rows staged in smem (Marlin's loop: A batch tile in smem, dequantized B in registers) cuts decode work 8× and lets tensor-core time (1.65 TFLOP / ~70 TFLOPS realistic mma.sync ≈ 24 ms at 8K) dominate. Register/smem budget on sm_121 is sufficient: 128×128 fp32 accumulators = 64 regs/thread over 256 threads; a 128-row × 128-k fp16 A stage = 32 KB, two stages = 64 KB < 99 KB. The decode-to-int8 crossover with the current kernel is ≈2K tokens; the fp16 dequant path never wins below ~3.5K. A persistent dequantized copy does not fit: int8 for 42 layers = 152 GB, NVFP4 ≈ 84 GB on top of ~76 GB of EXL3 weights per rank.

Two intermediate strategies exist in the literature only in dense form: (a) Marlin/Machete "weight-stationary in registers, activations in smem" (above) **[F]**; (b) decode-to-L2 slabs — no published kernel; **[I]** with a 12.6 MB/expert int8 slab and 1.8 GB of trellis streaming through L2 per layer, dirty-line write-back makes DRAM savings unreliable. A third, not a kernel change: **layer-major chunk ordering** for long prompts (for each layer, run all chunks; causal attention only needs earlier chunks' KV) amortises one dequant per layer across the whole prompt — for a 32K prompt in 2K chunks, 20 ms/16 ≈ 1.3 ms per layer-chunk. No source does this for MoE dequant; it is my inference and requires a custom prefill scheduler.

## Implications for our setup — ranked designs

1. **Weight-stationary fused trellis GEMM (decode once per B tile, stream 64–128 A rows).** Same y = ((x⊙su)H)W'(H⊙sv) math and codebook as today, so bit-identical output; only the mainloop changes (A_e rows for one expert staged in smem via cp.async, decoded B fragments held in registers across an M_blk pass, `m16n8k16` fp16 MMA; per-expert grid with a ticket scheduler like `exl3_moe`). Expected: **≈2× at 2K, ≈3.5–4× at 8K** (24→10–13 ms, 110→25–35 ms). Numerics risk: none. Effort: medium (inner-loop rewrite of `exl3_gemm_inner.cuh`; padding to M_blk multiples per expert costs only decode work, not DRAM). Confidence: high on direction, medium on magnitude (mma.sync fp16 with 99 KB smem realistically 60–75% of 100 TFLOPS).

2. **Rotated-domain W8A8 INT8 with per-chunk dequant-to-int8 (or, better, an int8-emitting trellis decoder).** Decode W' (rotated domain, Gaussian codebook values) to int8 with one per-tensor/per-row scale; gather + su-sign + H128 + per-token-per-128-group int8 quantization of x in one fused pass; int8 `mma.sync m16n8k32` grouped GEMM with int32→fp32 promotion per 128-k group; output H128 + sv in the epilogue. Expected error ≈1–1.5% (vs 6.3% fp8), backed by QuaRot INT8 (5.50 vs 5.47) and QoQ W4A8 (+0.05–0.15 ppl). Speed: at 2K chunks it only breaks even (≈23 ms) unless combined with layer-major chunk ordering (then ≈5–6× for ≥16K prompts); at 8K ≈3.5×. Effort: medium-high (three kernels; vLLM's Triton `int8_w8a8` path could serve as the GEMM initially). Confidence: high on numerics, medium on speed.

3. **Design 1 with INT8 MMA (hybrid).** Decode trellis directly to int8 fragments (the codebook is a finite 16-bit-state function, so an int8 LUT/bit-trick decode is possible), quantize A to int8 per (token, expert, 128-group) in the gather, use `m16n8k32.s8` at 2× the fp16 rate. Expected ≈20–25 ms at 8K, ≈8–10 ms at 2K. Numerics ≈1%. Effort: high. Confidence: medium.

4. **Fix the existing fp8 Triton path** (quantize in the rotated domain with 128-groups aligned to H128 blocks; tune B_M = 64, B_N = 128, B_K = 128, 3 stages within 99 KB). **[I]** Error should drop from 6.3% toward ≈4% and GEMM from 57 to maybe 90–120 TFLOPS, but it remains worse than int8 numerically and is still bounded by the 20 ms per-chunk dequant. Low effort; useful only as a stopgap.

5. **NVFP4 W4A4 via FlashInfer b12x / CUTLASS SM120 grouped block-scaled GEMM.** Works on GB10 (merged, 120–154 TFLOPS grouped measured) but requires re-quantizing 4-bit trellis weights to NVFP4/MXFP4 (double quantization) and 4-bit activations (W4A4 costs +0.5–0.6 ppl on Llama-2-7B even with full Hadamards). Not recommended for this checkpoint; the `kind::f8f6f4` mixed MXFP8×MXFP4 path has the same weight-requant problem.

6. **Convert experts to GPTQ/AWQ int4 and use Marlin MoE.** Marlin is ≈fp16-GEMM speed to M = 1024 and runs on sm_121, but it throws away the trellis quality advantage (QTIP 4-bit 5.17 vs GPTQ-class ≈5.6 on Llama-2-7B) and its ceiling is the same fp16 MMA ceiling as design 1.

Key hardware constraints to design around **[F]**: 99 KB smem/block, no TMEM/WGMMA/TMA-based warp specialization beyond what `sm_120f` CUTLASS provides, INT8 = FP8 rate ≈ 208 TFLOPS measured, BF16 ≈ 100 TFLOPS measured, 273 GB/s.
