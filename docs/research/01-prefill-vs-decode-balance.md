# Prefill/TTFT on 2× DGX Spark for GLM-5.3-Flash — research report

Conventions: **[F]** = fact with citation; **[I]** = my inference/derivation. Bandwidth used for derivations: 232 GB/s (measured saturating read), 273 GB/s spec.

## 0. Model correction (affects everything below)

**[F]** `zai-org/GLM-5.3-Flash/config.json`: `num_hidden_layers` 45, `hidden_size` 4096, `moe_intermediate_size` 2048, `n_routed_experts` 288, `num_experts_per_tok` 8, `n_shared_experts` 1, `first_k_dense_replace` 3, `kv_lora_rank` 512, `qk_rope_head_dim` 0, `v_head_dim` 256, `index_topk` 2048, `index_n_heads` 32, `index_head_dim` 128, `linear_attn_config: {num_heads 64, head_dim 128, short_conv_kernel_size 4}`, 34 KDA layers / 11 DSA layers, `num_nextn_predict_layers` 1, `max_position_embeddings` 1,048,576 ([config](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-5.3-Flash/raw/main/config.json)). The model card says 320B total / 18B active ([HF](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-5.3-Flash)); the vLLM recipe lists ~306 GiB FP8 weights ([recipes.vllm.ai](https://recipes.vllm.ai/zai-org/GLM-5.3-Flash)).

**[I]** Per expert 3·4096·2048 = 25.2M params → 7.25B routed params per MoE layer → **304B routed-expert params over 42 layers**. Active MoE FLOPs/token = 2·9·25.2M·42 ≈ **19 GFLOP/token** (about half of the ~36 GFLOP/token total; KDA q/k/v/o projections are ~5B params and are the bulk of the "dense GEMM" share). At 4-bit, routed experts are ~152 GB → **76 GB per GPU under TP2**.

## 1. Prefill vs decode: MoE roofline

**Derivation [I].** For one forward step of T tokens through E experts with top-k, per-expert params P_e, weight width b bytes:
- Bytes streamed ≈ E_touched(T)·P_e·b, with E_touched = E·(1−(1−k/E)^T). For k/E = 8/288, ≥99% of experts are touched once T ≳ 250, so bytes are constant ≈ E·P_e·b for any prefill chunk.
- FLOPs = 2·T·k·P_e.
- Arithmetic intensity on weights: AI = 2·T·k / (E·b). Compute-bound when AI ≥ ridge R = FLOPS_mma / BW, i.e.

  **T\* = R·E·b / (2k)**, equivalently **tokens-per-expert\* = R·b/2** (the M-dimension of each expert GEMM must exceed R·b/2).

- TP does not move T\*: TP2 halves bytes and FLOPs per GPU equally. It does halve absolute step time, at the cost of allreduce (see §5).
- R must use the FLOPS of the datatype the MMA actually executes in, not the storage dtype. On sm_121 there is no native FP4 tensor path in practice (§5), so 4-bit formats run bf16 or fp8 MMAs after dequant.

**Ridge points for GB10.** [F] Spec: 273 GB/s, "up to 1 PFLOP FP4" with sparsity ([NVIDIA](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/products/workstations/dgx-spark/)) → the usual ladder gives ~125 bf16 / ~250 fp8 dense TFLOPS peak [I]. [F] Measured: saturating-read 231–234 GB/s ([antirez/ds4 #773](https://github.com/antirez/ds4/issues/773)); FP8 GEMM ~86 TFLOPS reported by community microbenchmarks ([ai-muninn](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/dgx-spark-nvfp4-fp8-triton-patch), [forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/fp4-on-dgx-spark-why-it-doesnt-scale-like-youd-expect/360142)); LMSYS measured Llama-3.1-8B FP8 prefill at 7,991 tok/s ([LMSYS](https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2025-10-13-nvidia-dgx-spark/)), which implies ≥128 TFLOPS fp8 end-to-end [I]. I use "achievable" R with ~60 bf16 / ~120 fp8 TFLOPS over 232 GB/s.

| Weight format → MMA dtype | b (B/wt) | R theoretical (273 GB/s) | T\* theoretical | R achievable (232 GB/s) | T\* achievable | tokens/expert\* (achievable) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| bf16 → bf16 | 2 | 458 | 16.5K | 259 | 9.3K | 259 |
| fp8 → fp8 | 1 | 916 | 16.5K | 517 | 9.3K | 259 |
| EXL3 / INT4 → bf16 (dequant) | 0.5 | 458 | **4.1K** | 259 | **2.3K** | 65 |
| NVFP4 (+scales) → fp8 MMA | ~0.56 | 916 | 9.2K | 517 | 5.2K | 145 |
| NVFP4 → bf16 MMA | ~0.56 | 458 | 4.6K | 259 | 2.6K | 72 |
| hypothetical native FP4 MMA (500 TF) | 0.56 | 1832 | 18.5K | — | — | — |

Note the asymmetry: halving bytes lowers T\* (less to amortise), while a faster MMA dtype raises it. This matches the literature's "inflection around ~2k tokens per step" for MoE prefill ([ExFold](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24938), [AFD analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09721v1)).

**Applied to our EXL3/TP2 configuration [I]** (per GPU; t_bw = 76 GB / 232 GB/s = 0.33 s; t_mma = T·9.5 GFLOP / 60 TFLOPS):

| Chunk T | t_bw | t_mma (bf16) | MoE roofline step | MoE roofline tok/s | Whole-step roofline (MoE=63%) | Measured |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1K | 0.33 s | 0.16 s | 0.33 s (BW-bound) | 3,100 | ~2,000 | — |
| 2K | 0.33 | 0.32 | 0.33 (crossover) | 6,200 | ~3,900 | **900** |
| 4K | 0.33 | 0.63 | 0.63 | 6,500 | ~4,100 | — |
| 8K | 0.33 | 1.27 | 1.27 | 6,450 | ~4,100 | 1,800 (SGLang NVFP4) |
| 16K | 0.33 | 2.53 | 2.53 | 6,470 | ~4,100 | — |

Measured 900 tok/s at T=2048 means a 2.28 s step of which MoE ≈ 1.43 s versus a 0.33 s roofline: **the fused trellis MoE kernel runs ~4× below both the bandwidth and the tensor-core ceilings** (≈24 TFLOPS-equivalent, ≈107 GB/s-equivalent). Decode, by contrast, is on roofline: with 9 tokens/step (1+8 draft) only ~65 of 288 experts are touched → ~17 GB/GPU → ~85 ms/step; ×2–4.5 accepted tokens ≈ 25–55 tok/s, exactly what you measure [I].

**Why the MoE kernel is slow [I, medium confidence].** EXL3 stores 16×16 trellis tiles decoded procedurally ("3INST": LCG-style integer ops + conversion, plus a Hadamard un-rotation per tile) inside the GEMM ([exllamav3 doc](https://github.com/turboderp-org/exllamav3/blob/master/doc/exl3.md), [PonyExl3 notes](https://github.com/beamivalice/PonyExl3/blob/master/README.md)). That decode runs on CUDA cores (GB10: 48 SMs / 6,144 cores, ≈18 TFLOPS fp32 peak [I]). 152 G weights/GPU/step × ~20 ops ≈ 3 Tops ≈ 0.2–0.3 s if each weight is decoded **once**. With ~57 tokens per expert (T=2048) and 16-row MMA tiles, a kernel that re-decodes the weight tile for every M-tile does it ~4× → 0.8–1.2 s, which matches the observed 1.43 s. Test: MoE kernel time vs T should then be linear with a slope far above the tensor-core slope; if instead it is flat until ~2K it is BW-bound and the diagnosis is wrong. DeepSeek-V4-Flash (284B-A13B, 256 experts top-6, FP4 experts + FP8 dense, [NVIDIA NIM](https://docs.api.nvidia.com/nim/reference/deepseek-ai-deepseek-v4-flash)) has ~1.4× fewer active FLOPs/token; its 2.1× advantage means the Marlin/FP8 path is ~1.5× more FLOP-efficient than the EXL3 path on this box [I].

## 2. Chunked prefill in vLLM V1 / SGLang, and disaggregation

**vLLM V1 [F].** Chunked prefill is on by default; the scheduler "prioritizes decode requests" and batches all pending decodes before any prefill; `max_num_batched_tokens` low (~2048) → better ITL, high → better TTFT, >8192 recommended for throughput; setting it to `max_model_len` approximates V0 scheduling ([optimization.md](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/main/docs/configuration/optimization.md)). Concurrent partial prefills: `max_num_partial_prefills` (default 1), `max_long_partial_prefills` (default 1), `long_prefill_token_threshold` (default 4% of `max_model_len`); the PR reported p90 TTFT halved for medium prompts and "cut nearly 30×" for large ones with minimal throughput loss ([PR #10235](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/10235), [scheduler config](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/v0.15.0/api/vllm/config/scheduler/)). **[I]** With `max_model_len` = 1M the default long threshold is ~42K tokens, so your 30K prompts are classified "short" and compete FCFS with 150K ones — set it explicitly.

**SGLang [F].** `--chunked-prefill-size` (default derived, ~8K on large GPUs), `--max-prefill-tokens` (16384), `--schedule-policy` (fcfs default; `lpm` prefers cache hits), `--schedule-conservativeness`, `--enable-mixed-chunk` (prefill+decode in one batch), `--mamba-full-memory-ratio`, `--enable-unified-memory` for hybrids ([server args](https://docs.sglang.io/advanced_features/server_arguments.html)). Default policy is "prefill first, decode second"; maintainers suggest conservativeness 0.7–0.8, chunk 1–2K and `--max-running-requests` to protect decode ([discussion #27762](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/discussions/27762)). Qwen3.8 day-0 work added chunked pipeline-parallel prefill reaching 5,231 tok/s/GPU at 8K NVFP4 (datacenter GPUs) ([LMSYS](https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2026-08-12-qwen3-8-day0-support)).

**The chunk-size trade on this hardware [I].** Every chunk re-streams all 76 GB of expert weights (0.33 s). A 100K prompt in 2K chunks pays 50 × 0.33 = 16.5 s of pure weight traffic; in 8K chunks, 4.3 s. Meanwhile decode users see ITL ≈ one chunk time (2.3 s now; ~9 s at 8K with the current kernel). Chunk size is therefore a TTFT-vs-ITL dial whose cost is set by the kernel's T-scaling: fix the kernel first, then raise the chunk.

**Disaggregation [F].** DistServe (7.4× goodput / 12.6× tighter SLO; [arXiv 2401.09670](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09670)), Splitwise (1.4× throughput at 20% lower cost; [arXiv 2311.18677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18677)), Mooncake (KV-centric PD + chunked pipeline prefill for long prompts, early rejection; [arXiv 2407.00079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00079)). vLLM's docs state disaggregated prefill "DOES NOT improve throughput" and that chunked prefill with a proper chunk size achieves the same tail-latency goal ([disagg_prefill.md](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/main/docs/features/disagg_prefill.md)); SGLang PD supports hybrid models with an atomic Mamba-state transfer channel ([SGLang blog](https://pytorch.org/blog/hybrid-models-meet-sglang-more-than-full-attention/), [PD docs](https://docs.sglang.io/advanced_features/pd_disaggregation.html)). **[I]** PD is a non-starter here: a 152–170 GB model cannot fit on one 128 GB Spark, so both GPUs must hold half the weights and there is no second instance to disaggregate to. Its benefit (decode isolation) is already what vLLM V1's decode-first + chunking gives you.

## 3. Concurrency, KV/state budget, and TTFT under bursts

**Burst model [I].** With one prefill stream at throughput P and FCFS, the j-th of Q simultaneous requests gets TTFT_j = Σ_{i≤j} L_i / P. Four 100K prompts at 900 tok/s → 111 / 222 / 333 / 444 s; at 2,000 tok/s → 50 / 100 / 150 / 200 s. Round-robin partial prefills (`max_num_partial_prefills` = Q) makes everyone finish last; its value is letting **short** prompts overtake long ones (`max_long_partial_prefills` 1, explicit long threshold). Nothing but throughput or cache hits fixes the tail of an all-long burst — and a single uncached 100K prompt (measured 104 s on 2× Spark SGLang, [HF card](https://huggingface.co/randomllama/GLM-5.3-Flash-DFlash2-SGLang-2x-DGX-Spark)) already exceeds the Cloudflare window.

**State budget for this hybrid [I from config].**
- KDA state per sequence: 34 layers × 64 heads × 128×128 × 2 B ≈ 68 MiB (+~5 MiB conv) ≈ **73 MiB, fixed**; ~36 MiB per GPU under TP2 (heads split).
- MLA KV per token: 11 layers × 512 latent × 2 B = **11 KiB/token bf16** (5.5 KiB fp8; `qk_rope_head_dim` = 0 so there is no rope tail) plus indexer keys ~1.4 KiB/token. The latent is replicated across TP ranks. A 1M sequence = 10.7 GiB/GPU; 150K = 1.6 GiB.
- vs a pure-attention 30B-class GQA model (~200 KiB/token): ~18× smaller per token — this is the only reason 1M contexts fit at all. KDA state ≈ MLA KV of ~6.5K tokens, in line with vLLM's "roughly equivalent to the MLA cache for a few thousand tokens" for K3 [F: [vLLM K3 blog](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-07-27-k3)].

**The 4-sequence cap [I, medium-high].** vLLM V1 only requires the KV pool to hold one `max_model_len` request; `max_num_seqs` is a scheduler cap, and blocks are allocated as needed (with preemption when exhausted) ([issue #16118](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/16118)). With ~25–30 GiB of pool per GPU you have ~2.5M bf16 tokens (~5M fp8): enough for 4×1M **or** 25–50×100K. Raise `max_num_seqs` to 16–32; the KDA pool cost is 36 MiB/seq/GPU. In SGLang the Mamba pool (`--mamba-full-memory-ratio`, `--max-mamba-cache-size = concurrency × S`) sets the concurrency ceiling ([Qwen3.8 cookbook](https://docs.sglang.io/cookbook/autoregressive/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B)). Also consider dropping `max_model_len` to 256K: it shrinks the one-request reservation, CUDA-graph/profiling headroom, the indexer's per-chunk score buffers (T × L keys), and the default long-prefill threshold.

**[I]** The DSA indexer is the only quadratic term: 2·32·128·L²/2·11 layers ≈ 1,000 TFLOP at 150K (~6 s split over 2 GPUs at fp8 rates) — negligible now but it becomes dominant beyond ~500K.

## 4. Prefix caching for hybrid linear-attention models (status, 2026)

**vLLM [F].** Prefix caching for Mamba-2 hybrids landed as experimental ([PyTorch blog](https://pytorch.org/blog/hybrid-models-as-first-class-citizens-in-vllm/)); attention block size is auto-inflated to align with the Mamba page, so hits happen only at multiples of the aligned block — a 479-token prompt got ~0% and a 552-token prompt 95% on Qwen3.5 with a 528-token block ([issue #40696](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/40696)). Partial hits within a block via `prefix_match_unit` merged July 2026, but Mamba state is retained only at the last aligned boundary per block ([PR #46384](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/46384)). `--mamba-cache-mode align` is mandatory for GDN; tracking issue still lists LinearAttention/GDN align-mode items, Marconi-style caching (#37898), align-mode kernels (#40172) and Model Runner V2 fixes (#42406/#42792) as open ([#26201](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/26201)); an MRv2 + `mamba_cache_mode=all` + spec-decode crash was open Aug 2026 ([#52317](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/52317)). For KDA specifically, vLLM's Kimi K3 launch (July 2026) describes interval retention (`VLLM_PREFIX_CACHE_RETENTION_INTERVAL`, e.g. every 32K tokens plus prompt boundaries) and Marconi-style second-observation retention over "compact recurrent-state blocks for the KDA layers" ([vLLM K3 blog](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-07-27-k3)). **[I]** For GLM-5.3-Flash the KDA page (2 MiB/layer) versus 1 KiB/token/layer MLA KV implies an aligned block of ~2048 tokens (bf16 KV) or ~4096 (fp8 KV): system prompts shorter than that will never hit unless the fork carries the July partial-hit work. Whether your fork's base commit has the K3-era KDA caching needs checking.

**SGLang [F].** MambaRadixCache (Dec 2025) inserts checkpoints after chunked prefill / decode, separate LRU lists for state and KV, works with chunked prefill, spec decode and PD ([blog](https://pytorch.org/blog/hybrid-models-meet-sglang-more-than-full-attention/)); the Kimi K3 day-0 work added copy-on-write / snapshot / donate for KDA state with checkpoints prioritised at radix branching points ([LMSYS K3](https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2026-07-27-kimi-k3-day0-support/)); Qwen3.8 uses a Unified Radix Cache bundling GDN state + conv windows, checkpointing at prefill-chunk boundaries and decode intervals, integrated with HiCache ([LMSYS Qwen3.8](https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2026-08-12-qwen3-8-day0-support)). **[I]** SGLang's KDA prefix caching is the more mature of the two today; each cached prefix costs one ~36 MiB/GPU snapshot, so hundreds of cached agent prefixes are cheap. Multi-turn agents (append-only prompts) are the ideal case: the previous turn's end-state is exactly the next turn's prefix.

## Implications for our setup (prioritised)

1. **Stop the Cloudflare kills independently of the engine (high confidence, effect: eliminates the failure mode).** [F] 524 is a read/idle timeout on the origin response; Cloudflare's doc currently states a 125 s default, raisable to 6,000 s only on Enterprise, and suggests grey-clouding long endpoints ([CF docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/support/troubleshooting/http-status-codes/cloudflare-5xx-errors/error-524/)); community reports say the timer resets on any streamed bytes, so SSE heartbeat comments during prefill avoid it ([open-webui #16747](https://github.com/open-webui/open-webui/issues/16747), [CF community](https://community.cloudflare.com/t/are-server-sent-events-sse-supported-or-will-they-trigger-http-524-timeouts/499621)). Send headers immediately and emit `: ping` every ~20 s while queued/prefilling; disable proxy buffering.

2. **Fix the trellis MoE kernel's T-scaling (medium confidence on diagnosis; expected 1.5–2.5× prefill).** Instrument MoE kernel time vs chunk size. If linear with a steep slope, make the kernel weight-stationary: decode each 16×16 trellis tile once per step into shared memory/registers and sweep all token tiles of that expert over it. Roofline says MoE should be ~0.33 s at T=2K and ~1.3 s at 8K, versus 1.43 s now; after the fix, 8K chunks should reach ~2,000 tok/s whole-step, i.e. parity with the SGLang NVFP4 path. Do not spill dequantised experts to HBM (bf16 scratch traffic alone would be ~300 GB/step).

3. **Raise the chunk to 4–8K only after (2) (high confidence on direction).** Each chunk re-streams 76 GB; 8K chunks cut weight traffic on a 100K prompt from 16.5 s to 4.3 s. With the current kernel the gain is small and ITL for decode users rises to one chunk time.

4. **Scheduler knobs now (high confidence, cheap):** `max_num_seqs` 16–32 (KV pool is block-allocated; 4 is not required by 1M context), `long_prefill_token_threshold` ≈ 8192, `max_num_partial_prefills` 2–4 with `max_long_partial_prefills` 1 so 30K prompts overtake 150K ones (PR #10235 saw p90 TTFT halved to 30× better), `--scheduling-policy priority` for interactive traffic, and consider `max_model_len` 256K to reclaim reservation/indexer-buffer memory. Add admission control: queue or reject prompts whose predicted TTFT (Σ queued tokens / P) exceeds the SLO, per Mooncake.

5. **Prefix caching for KDA state (medium confidence; effect: TTFT → near zero for repeated system prompts and agent turns).** Verify the fork carries vLLM's KDA retention (`--enable-prefix-caching --mamba-cache-mode align`, retention interval); expect a ~2K-token (bf16 KV) / ~4K (fp8 KV) hit granularity, so pad or structure system prompts to aligned boundaries. SGLang's Unified Radix Cache is more mature for KDA today; if you keep the SGLang NVFP4 deployment for long-prompt/agent traffic, enable radix cache with `--schedule-policy lpm`.

6. **Enable GPUDirect RDMA for NCCL over the ConnectX-7 link (medium confidence, ~3–4% step time).** Allreduce is ~7% of the step at 10 GB/s; GDR-on has been reported near 2× that.

7. **fp8 KV cache (`--kv-cache-dtype fp8`, supported on Blackwell per vLLM recipe; works on GB10 in SGLang per the HF card):** halves MLA KV/token, doubling admissible concurrency at 100K+, at the cost of coarser prefix-cache alignment.

8. **Not worth pursuing:** PD disaggregation (no room for a second instance), expecting NVFP4 tensor-core speedups (none on sm_121), or CUTLASS FP4 paths without a correctness check.

Confidence summary: roofline formula and crossover table — high; MoE-kernel diagnosis (decode-per-M-tile) — medium, testable in one profiling run; KDA state/KV sizes — high (from config); 4-seq cap being an artifact — medium-high; prefix-cache granularity of ~2K tokens for this model — medium (derived, not measured).
