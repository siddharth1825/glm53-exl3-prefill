Verdict: the prefill was kernel-bound, not bandwidth-bound, and the exact-numerics fix is a weight-stationary trellis GEMM. Built, gated and serving: cold prefill 922 → 1,500 tok/s, TTFT at 32K 27.5 → 16.9 s.

The fused MoE kernel runs at roughly 13–15% of the GB10's bf16 tensor peak because it decodes every weight tile again for every 16 activation rows. Decoding each tile once and sweeping 64–128 rows over it is the design Marlin uses for int4; nobody has shipped it for trellis weights. Estimated MoE time per layer drops from 24 to ~11 ms at 2K-token chunks and from 110 to ~30 ms at 8K, with no change to the model's numerics. Before building it, pull the upstream image: it already carries a +20% fat-expert kernel we do not have.

The numbers that bound the design

Per tensor-parallel rank, per MoE layer: 3.62 G expert parameters, which is 1.81 GB at 4 bpw (6.6 ms of DRAM time), 3.62 GB as int8 or fp8 (13.3 ms), 7.2 GB as fp16 (26.5 ms). Work is 201 MFLOP per token, so 0.41, 0.82 and 1.65 TFLOP at 2K, 4K and 8K tokens.

Step anatomy from the image authors' profile at 1024-token chunks: MoE forward 63%, dense GEMMs 10%, NCCL allreduce 7%, KDA 6%, sparse attention 4%. On our box the mHC hyper-connection kernels are confirmed fused (TileLang JIT is active on sm_121), so they are not a hidden cost.

Why the earlier attempts stalled

M-tiling to 64 rows in the same kernel

Correct but not faster. It kept the existing structure: B decoded per pass into a register file already at 128 registers with spills, 33% occupancy and 31% of stalls on barriers. Design 1 is not a bigger M inside the same loop. It is Marlin's loop: the activation tile stationary in shared memory, decoded weight fragments held across the pass, a deep cp.async pipeline. The budget fits: 128×128 fp32 accumulators are 64 registers per thread across 256 threads, and two 128-row × 128-k fp16 activation stages take 64 KB of the 99 KB.

Occupancy and pipeline-depth variants

+10–14% on the kernel, +3% end to end. That confirms the kernel is stall-bound rather than decode-throughput-bound, so only a mainloop redesign changes it.

fp8 dequant-once

6.3% error because the activations were quantized before the Hadamard rotation, and the scratch traffic makes the path a net loss below about 4K tokens regardless.

Plan, kernel scope

  1. Redeploy from the upstream image with PR77 (EXL3_FAT_KERNEL=1, chunk size 7168) and re-run our benchmark harness so the +20% and the larger chunk are measured on our box rather than assumed.

    The image also carries the reasoning-effort prefix-cache fix (#63) and the indexer workspace right-sizing (#86).

  2. Read exl3_fat_gemm.cu in that image before writing anything. If E2 already stages activation rows in shared memory, design 1 is an extension of it; if it still re-decodes per 16 rows, design 1 replaces its mainloop.

  3. Build design 1 as a mainloop rewrite in the op harness first. Gate: fp16-noise equality with the reference loop, then the logprob/KLD gate on the served model.

    Temperature-0 diffs are not a usable gate here: the stock engine reproduces only 10 of 12 prompts against itself.

  4. Decide on int8 (designs 2 and 3) only afterwards, and only if design 1 lands short of ~25 ms at 8K and the KLD budget allows ~1% activation error.

  5. Do not spend time on fp8 W8A8 (numerics), NVFP4 re-quantization (quality), Marlin conversion (quality), or dequantizing to memory (bandwidth).

Findings outside the kernel scope

Each of these addresses the original time-to-first-token complaint without touching model capability. Listed, not acted on.

  • --long-prefill-token-threshold 1024: on this exact stack, concurrent sessions froze 440 s behind one long prefill and dropped to 18–25 s with the flag, at ~10% cost to the long prefill (MiaAI issue #110). vLLM V1 has no other partial-prefill control.
  • MAX_NUM_SEQS=4 is the recipe's choice, not an engine limit. 8–12 is safe; above 12 hits the MTP profiling bug (#88). The KV pool is block-allocated, so a 1M context does not reserve 1M tokens per sequence.
  • Cloudflare 524: SSE heartbeat comments during prefill reset the 100 s timer; grey-clouding the host removes it entirely.
  • DFlash2 turns negative beyond about 6 concurrent streams on two GB10s; num_speculative_tokens_per_batch_size tapers the draft length with batch size. The drafter checkpoint is CC BY-NC-ND.
  • Spec-decode drafter state fragments the hybrid KDA+MLA cache and can halve long-prefill speed after mixed workloads; --mixed-prefill-token-cap -1 helped in the NVIDIA forum thread.
  • Expert parallelism (EP=2) instead of tensor parallelism for the experts keeps full 2048-wide expert shapes and replaces two allreduces per layer with one all-to-all. The 2× RTX PRO 6000 EXL3 recipe runs this way at 4,200–6,200 tok/s. Worth a look after design 1.
  • Prefix caching on this hybrid hits only at 2304–3584-token page boundaries in align mode; --prefix-match-unit and the retention interval control it.
  • NCCL runs at roughly 10 GB/s effective without GPUDirect RDMA; allreduce is about 7% of a step.

Open questions

  • The realistic fp16 mma.sync efficiency on GB10 with 99 KB of shared memory. The design assumes 60–75% of 100 TFLOPS; the first kernel prototype answers this.
  • Whether PR77's E2 kernels already stage activation rows in shared memory.
  • Which KDA backend our image actually runs on sm_121 (Triton FLA assumed; 6% of the step for 0.7% of the FLOPs).
